# Supported seated story acting — source10

`performance-2.2.10`, `story-direction-2.2.22` and `physical-seat-2.2.1` add physical seated acting to the common renderer. Sitting is optional choreography for a motivated story situation; it is not a required scene format, costume, palette or presenter behavior. Narration, source quotations and audio clock remain authoritative.

## Implemented contract

- `performance.supports` declares passive seats: `{id,kind:"seat",center:{x,y},width,facing:"left"|"right",backHeight?}`. Center is the world-space seat top/pelvis contact anchor; feet stay at the ground anchor around the performer root. Both rig kinds use their own fixed thigh/shin dimensions. The renderer draws the support in the same camera world as actor, floor and objects; optional backHeight adds a backrest.
- `entryPosture` or a `postures` target can use `{pose:"seated",supportId}`. Seated intensity must be omitted or 1, so a held seated pose settles fully on the support. Other poses cannot own supportId. Sit/stand transitions take at least700ms and complete before locomotion or body turning. Lean changes while remaining on the same seat take at least280ms. Seated gaze, expression and hand work remain available. Changing seats requires standing first.
- The compiler keeps feet planted and all bone lengths fixed. A left/right knee-pole change travels through a straight-leg waypoint, rather than flipping the knee. Sampling/refinement includes transition midpoints; the emitted JavaScript remains declarative GSAP with the existing transform allowlist. No callbacks, wall clock, DOM scripting or security relaxation was added.
- Support geometry must fit the stage, support both hips and produce reachable, forward thighs and grounded shins. Every actor shares stage dimensions and ground. Duplicate/changing support definitions, simultaneous owners of one platform, overlapping seats, wrong facing and invalid sit/walk/turn schedules fail validation. Ownership covers sit/stand transitions, not only the held contact frames. Geometry comparison ignores JSON key order and treats an omitted backHeight as zero.
- Primary and supporting actors can each own supported choreography. Continuous cuts retain posture, actor/root/scale and the exact occupied support geometry. Ensemble camera checks include the seat, backrest and floor; intentional face/contact close framing can crop the support while the world anchor remains valid.
- Studio's performance review shows each actor's posture sequence and seat count. API/CLI/storyboard JSON and exported schemas use the same fields. Reports include seat supports, support renderer version and maximum compiled pelvis/contact error. These values are producer measurements, not independent browser/seek tests.

Animation7/8/9 without seat data remains parseable; version9 retains hand-track support. Seat data requires version10. Current direction/runtime literals still trigger explicit locked-plan migration gates. The approved static rig identity remains independent of these runtime versions. Project/scene visual fingerprints include the seat renderer version; narration fingerprints do not.

For an initial layout with nearly horizontal thighs, place the seat about one upper-leg length behind planted feet and one lower-leg length above ground. Adjust to the actual rig and intended pose; geometric validation remains authoritative. A supported body posture does not establish an unsourced historical event. A symbolic patent document or research situation must keep its illustrative provenance and sourced date/person labels.
